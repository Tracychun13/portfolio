Digital Deathmatch (aka) RoboCage

Robo Cage started as a summer co-op project with a team of three: Lorenzo, Yang and me. We later continued it in one of our Game Design and Development classes focused on passion projects, where we were lucky to have another 3D artist, Chris, join the team. The name started out as RoboCage but we later changed it to be Digital Deathmatch.

My role
UI/UX Designer & 3D Artist
Timeline
June 2025 – Present
Tools
Unity (ECS/DOTS), C#, Maya
Team
Team of 4 (Hybrid)

The problem

This was the first time any of us had worked on a live, server-based multiplayer game. Learning to work within Unity's ECS architecture was a challenge, and we were a bit ambitious about how much we could accomplish in such a short time.

The goal was for the game to hold at least 6 players at a time.

How we got there

The process

The result

Robo Cage is still in the works but players can join a lobby, connect with friends and battle in the arena. Our team is continuing to develop it, but the core experience is in place, and seeing our separate pieces of code, art and UI come together into one game was one of the most rewarding parts of the project.

  • Designed and implemented the game's full interactable UI, connected to live gameplay data so players always know their health, ammo and match status at a glance.
  • Modeled the game's weapons and arena environment assets, shaping the look and feel of the map.
  • Presented our progress to a class of 30 each sprint and used their feedback to refine the game.
  • Built a working online multiplayer foundation as a team, using server-authoritative networking, client-side prediction, and Unity Lobby and Relay.

What I learned

I wasn't very confident in my UI design skills going into this project, and it really pushed me to grow. It also made me a faster 3D modeler and reminded me just how time consuming the modeling process can be. The creative freedom I had let me experiment with the map's aesthetics and design, and it was amazing to watch all the pieces come together. Looking ahead, I want our team to be more organized. Coordinating four people's schedules made it hard to meet up and break down everything we needed to tackle, which makes sense since most of us were swamped with different classes throughout the whole semester.
